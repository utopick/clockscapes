Clockscapes privacy policy

Last updated on 25 November 2026.

Clockscapes is a free world clock made by utopick. This page explains what happens to your information when you use it, both the web version at utopick.github.io/clockscapes and the Android app. The short answer is that Clockscapes collects nothing about you: it has no account, no ads, no analytics and no tracking, and your settings never leave your device.

What stays on your device

Your clocks, alarms, music and screen settings are saved only on your device, in the browser's storage or in the Android app's own storage. Nobody else can see them, including us, and they are deleted when you clear the site's data or uninstall the app.

Clockscapes never asks for your location. It shows your local time from your device's clock and time zone, which it reads on the device and doesn't send anywhere.

What is sent to the weather service

To show the live weather, Clockscapes asks Open-Meteo, a free weather service, for the forecast and the air quality at the coordinates of the cities you picked. When you press FIND to look up a place by name, the name you typed is sent to Open-Meteo's place search. These requests contain the cities' coordinates or the typed name, never anything about you, but like any request on the internet they reach Open-Meteo from your device's IP address. Open-Meteo's own privacy policy explains how it handles them. You can switch the live weather off in the SCREEN tab, and then Clockscapes sends no requests at all.

Where the web version comes from

The web version is hosted on GitHub Pages, so opening it loads the page from GitHub's servers, which see your IP address as any website does. GitHub's privacy statement covers that. The Android app carries the page inside it, so it doesn't load it from GitHub.

What the Android app's permissions are for

The app asks for the internet to fetch the weather, for notifications to show the music controls and your alarms, and for exact alarms so each alarm rings on time, even after the phone restarts. Playing music in the background uses a media playback service, which shows a notification while it plays. The app can also be your live wallpaper, the TV's screensaver and a music app in Android Auto. None of these collect or send any information.

Children

Clockscapes collects no personal information from anyone, children included.

Changes and contact

If this policy changes, the new version will be on this page with a new date. Clockscapes is open source, so you can read its code on GitHub. For any question, open an issue there at github.com/utopick/clockscapes/issues.
