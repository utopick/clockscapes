▚ CLOCKSCAPES

Weather and places

Weather data by Open-Meteo.com, under the Creative Commons Attribution 4.0 licence (CC BY 4.0). Clockscapes changes the data: it turns the current conditions into the sky, rain, snow and fog of each scene, and shows the temperature and a short description.

The FIND button's place search uses the Open-Meteo geocoding API, which is based on GeoNames (CC BY 4.0). The free Open-Meteo API is for non-commercial use, and Clockscapes is free.

The app

Clockscapes is open source under the MIT licence. The clock font is Nunito, copyright 2014 The Nunito Project Authors, under the SIL Open Font License 1.1. The city coordinates are adapted from countries_cities_data. The pictures and the music are made by the app's own code.

Screen

A filter over the clocks that imitates an old screen. CRT looks like a tube television, with scanlines, glowing colours and dark rounded corners. LCD shows the thin gaps between the pixels of a handheld screen. Save to see it.

Power and screen care

Battery saver draws 5 frames a second instead of 15, so the phone stays cooler. Letting the screen sleep saves the most. Burn-in protection keeps OLED screens safe when the clocks stay up for hours: the picture moves by a pixel or two every 2 minutes, and a soft line sweeps down the screen every 10 minutes.

Android

Show these clocks on your home screen and lock screen as a live wallpaper. Your phone adds its own clock on the lock screen, which Clockscapes can't hide, so you may want to make it smaller or hide it in the phone's lock screen settings. Some phones only let you change its style, and on Samsung phones the free Good Lock app (its LockStar part) can hide it.

Music

Lo-fi music written live in this page, so nothing is downloaded. Each song comes from one of your clocks in turn: its city and its hour choose the song, and its sun sets the mood, so the night is slower and darker than the day. When it rains there, you hear the rain. After two or three minutes a song fades into the next clock's song. The same variation number at the same hour plays the same songs again, and a new number gives you new ones. The music button starts the variation of the time it's pressed, so at 15:32 you hear variation 1532 (a random time when none of your clocks is in this device's time zone).

Alarms (this device’s time)

Each alarm goes off every day at this device’s time, and for a minute its message crosses your clocks, one word per truck or sign: neon trucks where it is night and a march where it is day. With the clocks side by side it drives from one clock into the next along the same road, and stacked in bands every clock shows it (by day a band may get banner planes instead). Tap any clock to stop it. The sound can only play once you have tapped or clicked the page since opening it.

The app also rings each alarm as a phone alarm, with a notification and the alarm sound, even when the app is closed. The message only crosses the clocks while the app or the wallpaper is on screen.

To try it, save, then press A three times, or swipe down, up and down again on the clocks in one stroke.
